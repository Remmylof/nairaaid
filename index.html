The problem

Help is generous. The path it takes is not.

⇄

Middlemen

Donations in Nigeria often pass through several hands: a coordinator, an account holder, a cash handout. Each step is a chance for "admin costs", padded lists or diversion.

⏱

Late arrival

School fees have deadlines and rent is due monthly. Help that arrives weeks late, after queues and manual approvals, often arrives too late to matter.

?

No checkable record

Donors, especially in the diaspora, get a WhatsApp screenshot at best. There's no record anyone can check, and leftover funds quietly stay with the organiser.

      Why Web3

      Rules in code, not promises

      Nobody can bend the rules

      Stipend amounts, periods, milestones and the recipient cap are fixed when the campaign is created. Not even the organiser can change them.

      Checkable by anyone

      Every donation, verification, payout and refund is a public event. A donor in London sees the same record as a student in Ilorin.

      Naira, not volatile crypto

      cNGN is a regulated, 1:1 naira-backed stablecoin on Celo. Recipients think and spend in naira, and Celo fees are a fraction of a cent.

      Private by design

      Only anonymous IDs and wallets go on-chain. Vulnerable people aren't exposed, but ghost beneficiaries are much harder to create.

      How it works

      One contract: NairaAidCampaigns.sol on Celo

      This demo mirrors the Solidity contract in the repository. It is an unaudited hackathon prototype, deployed to testnets only.

      1. Create a campaigncreateCampaign fixes the stipend per period, number of periods, milestones (e.g. school fees ₦50,000), the recipient cap and an eligibility document.
      2. Fund itfund() records every donation per donor. The optional organiser fee (capped at 3%) is paid on top, so recipients always get the full rule amount.
      3. 2-verifier registrationregisterRecipient activates an ID only when two different verifiers attest the same anonymous ID and the same wallet. The organiser can never be a verifier.
      4. Stipend periodsclaimStipend pays each period that has started, exactly once, and only to the recipient's registered wallet.
      5. MilestonesconfirmMilestone releases a payout when a second, different verifier confirms. One verifier can't confirm twice.
      6. Close & pro-rata refundscloseCampaign snapshots what's unspent; claimRefund returns each donor's share. There is no withdraw or sweep function at all.

      Roadmap & sustainability

      From a demo to real students

      Now

      Hackathon prototype

      Contract, 32 tests, end-to-end demo, testnet deploy scripts, this interactive site.

      Next

      Pilot: 20–50 students

      One campus in Ilorin with faculty officers and the student union as verifiers, a simple verifier app and bank off-ramp.

      Then

      2–3 NGOs + USSD

      Partner NGOs and faith-based funds run their own campaigns. USSD access so recipients on feature phones can check and cash out.

      Sustainability

      • Optional organiser fee, capped at 3% and paid on top by donors, never taken from recipients.
      • Ecosystem and social-impact grants for development and pilots.
      • Low running costs: one shared contract, Celo gas measured in fractions of a cent.

      Risks we're honest about

      • Verifier collusion: reduced by requiring two independent verifiers, not removed.
      • Off-ramp access and stablecoin regulation can change.
      • Smart-contract risk: unaudited; an audit comes before real funds.
      • Digital literacy: USSD and agents needed for people without smartphones.
